I read an argument recently I haven't stopped thinking about: AI is making average work free.

Every team I know can now produce a decent first draft of almost anything. Code, docs, decks, plans. And that is exactly why "decent" stopped being worth paying for.

The scarce thing is no longer execution. It is having a point of view worth executing.

The better these models get at knowing everything the world already thinks, the more valuable it becomes to think something the world doesn't. Contrarian. Specific. Slightly wrong in a way only you would be.

That doesn't come from better prompting. It comes from a self with opinions, tastes and refusals — and from feeding it inputs nobody in your feed is reading.

Most of us were trained out of that. School rewarded the right answer. Early jobs rewarded not being difficult. It works, right up until everyone has the same tools and the only differentiator left is what you choose to say.

I have watched this in hiring for years. The engineers who stood out were never the fastest typists. They were the ones who disagreed, with evidence, at the right moment.

So the question isn't how to use AI better. It's whether you still have a thought that isn't the average of your training data.

If you do, AI just made you ten times more useful. If you don't, AI just made you ten times more replaceable.

Same tool. Different starting point.
